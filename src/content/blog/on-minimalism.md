---
title: "Minimalism as methodology"
date: 2026-03-01
excerpt: "Why removing things is harder than adding them, in research and in life."
---

Every addition to a system is a hypothesis about what matters. Every removal is a proof.

In research, I've found that the most productive phase of any project is the one where you start deleting. The initial architecture is always too complex — you add modules because they might help, conditions because they might matter, losses because they might stabilize training. Then you spend weeks discovering which of these additions were load-bearing and which were decorative.

The same principle applies to how I structure my life, my music, my tools. A Eurorack system with 200 modules isn't more capable than one with 20 — it's more confusing. The constraint of fewer modules forces you to understand each one deeply, to find the music that lives in the interactions rather than the individual parts.

This website is built on the same principle. One typeface. Two colors. No animations. Every element earns its place by being necessary, not by being possible.
